# Future of Work in Agentic Laboratories: Open Science, Health Scientists









## Two objects, and only one of them gets measured today

Open science is a property of what comes out of the laboratory. The health of the scientists is a property of the people it came out of. Almost all of the current writing about agents in research measures the first and assumes the second arrives with it. A laboratory can raise its output and lose the people who produced it, and the publication record will look identical either way for about two years.

The unit here is a research group, not a facility. The subjects are graduate students and postdoctoral researchers, in Computer Science first and then across the College of Engineering, and they are the driving force of the study as well as its subjects. That arrangement is right for the science and it is the hardest one for a consent model, which is the part I would settle first.

## Why a collector and not a survey

Self-report is not a usable instrument here, and the reason is a measurement result rather than a suspicion. In a randomized trial, experienced developers working on real issues in repositories they maintain forecast that their tools would make them faster, estimated afterwards that the tools had made them faster, and were measured to be slower. Two numbers produced by the person who did the work, and the sign is wrong in both.

A second experiment says what the quantity actually is. Engineers learning an unfamiliar library under assistance finished in about the same time and understood less afterwards, with the largest drop in debugging, and the authors separate six interaction patterns of which three preserve the learning outcome even under assistance. So the effect of an agent on a researcher is not a property of the researcher and not a property of the tool. It is a property of the interaction, session by session, and it changes within a person across a day. A person cannot report a pattern they were not aware of adopting. The interaction is the only place the quantity exists.

The same record is what open science is missing from the other direction. A survey of autonomous research agents found that code release is now standard practice while the artifacts that let somebody re-run a result are not, and the missing artifact is the execution trace. One substrate, two obligations, and they do not compete. That is why the title puts open science and healthy scientists on the same line.

## The instrument, and why it takes five years

[Agentic Lab](../../../artifacts/agentic-lab/) is the collection-and-analysis layer: a typed event schema, multi-provider ingestion adapters, a provenance graph annotated along teamwork, cognition, science quality, wellbeing and organizational dynamics, and weekly diagnostics for data quality and drift, with a consent gate under all four and the protocol version stored on every record.

Five years rather than one, for three reasons. The claim is longitudinal by construction, and a cohort of graduate students takes four to six years to pass through. The instrument has to survive its own revisions, which is what the stored protocol version buys. And the thing being measured is moving fast enough that a one-year study measures one point on a slope whose direction was the question.

The vocabulary the collector needs before it collects anything, including the three things called a trace across my own files, is on the [Lexicon](lexicon.md) page.
