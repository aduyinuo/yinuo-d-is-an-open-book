# Lexicon

The phrase at the centre of this vista already means something else one project over. In my own files, competitive learning names a machine procedure: a set of strategies that improves by being made to face itself, which is the loop [AcceleratePSRO](../../3-year-agenda/cyber-world-modeling/accelerate-psro.md) runs and speeds up. On the vista page it names people learning by competing, and a competition used as a test of what they learned. Both uses are mine, both are current, and the second one is the one that broke.

The overlap is more than a nuisance. The machine sense carries the quantity the human sense has lost. Policy space response oracles have a name for a competition whose entrants have stopped separating, a name for what that costs the analysis, and a name for the repair. This page borrows all three and says where the analogy stops holding.

## The census

_**Competitive cybersecurity training**_, on the [Problem-Solving](../../3-year-agenda/mental-world-modeling/problem-solving/) index, where the question is how to ensure its effectiveness in the era of agent solvers. The human sense.

_**Competitive tier**_, on the vista page, in the sentence doing the most work: every team in it solved every hard challenge. Also the human sense.

_**Strategy population**_, on the AcceleratePSRO page, in the sentence about assembling a population that represents the full game under a computational budget. The machine sense. No people in it, and the competition is internal to an algorithm.

_**The era of agent solvers**_, where the two senses meet. The competitors are people, the thing that changed is a machine, and the claim is about what a competition should be scored on once the machine is in the room.

_**Visible problem-solving traces**_, which is neither sense. That is the replacement instrument being built, and it is scored on process rather than on outcome by construction, since a writeup recording only the flag is discarded by the pipeline as carrying no evidence.

## The two competitions

_**Competitive learning.**_ A scheme in which the improvement of each participant is driven by facing the others, so that what any one of them learns depends on the set they are placed against. Not individual practice against a fixed curriculum, where the difficulty schedule is authored in advance and does not respond to how well anyone is doing. The test: ask who the opponent is and whether the opponent moves. A scored ranking against other people, updated as they improve, is the human sense. A set of strategies expanded by best response against the current mixture is the machine sense. A fixed answer key is neither.

On the vista page the participants are teams of students, the payoff is a score on a leaderboard, and the reason to run the competition at all is that the ranking was believed to identify talent. Nothing is being optimised by the organisers. The competition is a measuring device pointed at a population that arrived on its own. In AcceleratePSRO the participants are policies, the payoff is an entry in an empirical payoff matrix, and the reason to run the loop is to produce the population. So the same words name a device for reading a population and a device for manufacturing one. A sentence claiming that competitive learning is effective means, in the first case, that people who compete end up more able, and in the second, that the restricted game converges toward the full game. Neither claim supports the other, and this vista is a case where the first failed while the second was doing fine.

_**Competition as instrument.**_ A competition treated as a measuring device: the score is read as an estimate of a person's ability, and decisions about admission, hiring and scholarship are taken on that reading. Not a competition treated as a training event, where the score is feedback to the participant and nobody outside the room acts on it. The same event can be both, and the failure described on the vista page hits the instrument role while leaving the training role partly intact. The test: does anyone who was not in the competition make a decision on the basis of its ranking?

## The break, and what it is a break in

_**The break.**_ A discontinuity in a population-scale time series large enough that measurements taken before and after it cannot be pooled, because the quantity being measured is no longer the same quantity. Not a trend, which is a change in the value of a stable measurement, and not an outlier year, which reverts. A break is identified by the fact that the measurement procedure has changed meaning, not by the size of the jump. The test: would you be willing to fit one model across the boundary?

Six years on one platform, so the population and the challenge authoring process are held roughly fixed across the boundary. That is what makes the break attributable to something outside the competition rather than to a change in who entered.

_**Score compression.**_ The collapse of the spread of scores across a ranked field, to the point where the ranking is decided by a tie-breaker rather than by the scored quantity. Not grade inflation, where scores rise and the spread is preserved, and not a ceiling effect on a single item, where one task saturates while the rest of the instrument still separates. Compression is a property of the whole instrument. Compute the variance of the score across the top of the field; if it is at or near zero and the published ranking is nevertheless ordered, find out what the order is actually made of. Here the answer is completion time, and a ranking produced entirely by a tie-breaker is a ranking on the tie-breaker.

_**Discriminative power.**_ The extent to which an instrument assigns different scores to people who differ on the thing it is meant to measure. Not validity, which asks whether the thing being measured is the intended one, and not reliability, which asks whether the same person would score the same twice. An instrument can be perfectly reliable, produce the same score for everyone, and have no discriminative power at all. This is the quantity the vista says was lost, and naming it this way keeps the argument narrow. I am not claiming the competitors are less able than their predecessors. I am claiming the instrument can no longer tell me either way, which is a weaker claim and a worse problem.

_**Hard is a design label on one side of the break and a measured property on the other.**_ When the field-wide solve rate on hard challenges triples, the word hard has not changed: it is still the label the challenge author applied at authoring time, on the basis of what the author expected the field to be able to do. What changed is the field. A sentence like the hard challenges got easier is therefore false and the wrong shape. Any measure of difficulty defined as a solve rate is a measure of a population and a challenge set jointly, and it will report a change in the population as a change in the challenge. That is the reason the [Capture-the-Flag](../../3-year-agenda/mental-world-modeling/problem-solving/capture-the-flag.md) work asks what makes a challenge difficult rather than which challenges are difficult. The first question has an answer that survives a break; the second does not.

## Performance, expertise, and three ways a skill can be missing

_**Performance.**_ What a person produces on a task, under whatever conditions and with whatever assistance the task allows. Not expertise, which is a property of the person and is supposed to persist when the conditions change. Performance is a joint property of a person, their tools and the task, and the whole vista is about the widening of the difference. The test: change the conditions and re-measure. Whatever survives the change was expertise.

_**Expertise maintenance.**_ Keeping a skill available in a workforce over time, against decay in the individuals who hold it and against loss of the conditions under which new people would acquire it. Not training, which is about acquisition, and not retention in the personnel sense, which is about keeping people. Maintenance is about keeping the skill, and it can fail while the roster is full. The test: ask where the next person who has this skill is going to come from, and what they will practice on. If the answer is a task that is now routinely delegated, the skill is not being maintained regardless of how many people currently hold it.

_**Deskilling, and its two neighbours.**_ Deskilling proper is the loss of a skill a person previously held, following a period in which the skill was not exercised because something else performed it. Skill formation failure is the case where the skill was never acquired, because the learner delegated during the window in which they would have built it. Ordinary decay is the case where the skill lapses through disuse with no substitute involved. The three have different populations, different remedies, and different evidence. Ask whether the person ever had it. If yes and the lapse follows exposure to a substitute, deskilling. If no and the substitute was present during learning, formation failure. If yes and no substitute is involved, decay. My reading table holds all three under one heading and does not make the distinction, which is a defect this page fixes rather than the table. The endoscopy result is deskilling in the strict sense. The controlled study on developers learning an unfamiliar asynchronous library is formation failure, since nobody in either arm knew the library at the start, and the finding my page cites from it, that assistance impaired conceptual understanding, code reading and debugging without significant average efficiency gains, is a statement about acquisition and not about loss.

The two costs run on different clocks and only one of them shows up in a competition. Deskilling is fast to observe and reversible in principle, because the person can be put back on the task. Formation failure is slow to observe and expensive to reverse, because there is no earlier state to return to and the window in which the skill would have been built has closed. A competition sees neither directly. It sees performance, which both leave intact or improve. That is why the vista does not argue from the scores at all after the first section: the scores are the symptom that something is unobservable, not the evidence of what happened.

## Knowing how well you are doing

_**Calibration.**_ Agreement between a person's estimate of their own performance and their measured performance. Not confidence, which is the estimate alone, and not accuracy, which is the performance alone. Calibration is defined only when both are in hand, which is why it is the first casualty of an instrument that has stopped separating people. Collect the forecast before the task and the estimate after it, then compare both against the measurement. Two gaps, and they can point in different directions.

_**Control-arm attrition.**_ Systematic loss of participants or tasks from the arm of a trial that withholds the treatment, produced by the participants' unwillingness to be in that arm. Not ordinary dropout, which is not selective on the treatment, and not non-compliance, where the participant stays and violates the protocol. Attrition of this kind biases the estimate rather than only shrinking it, and the direction of the bias is knowable. The follow-up to the developer productivity study ran into exactly this and said so: a significant increase in developers declining to participate because they did not wish to work without the tools, with a large share of those who did take part holding back the tasks they expected the tools to help most on. The follow-up estimates were withdrawn as unreliable and the design is being changed.

That entry belongs in a lexicon rather than a footnote because it is the vista's argument arriving early and in the methods section. The ability to measure what a person can do without assistance is itself a resource, and it is being spent. A five-year program that plans to answer these questions by randomising the tools has to assume that the control arm will be harder to fill every year it waits.

_**Process measurement.**_ Scoring a person on the sequence of steps by which they reached an answer, rather than on whether the answer was reached. Not outcome measurement, which reads only the final state, and not time-to-outcome, which reads the final state and a clock and is still an outcome measure. A tie-broken leaderboard is an outcome measure with a clock. The test: could two participants who both reached the flag receive different scores? If not, the instrument is reading outcomes only, and it will saturate as soon as everyone reaches the flag.

## What PSRO lends, and where the loan stops

Empirical game-theoretic analysis has been staring at a competition that stopped separating its entrants for twenty years, because that is the condition under which its main loop terminates. The vocabulary transfers further than I expected it to, and the places it fails are informative.

_**Best response, and the response oracle.**_ The subroutine that takes a distribution over the opponents currently in the population and returns a strategy that does as well as possible against it. The loop alternates between solving the restricted game for a mixture and calling the oracle against that mixture, and the cost of one call dominates the cost of the whole procedure. A competition has this shape read from the other side. The challenge set is the mixture, each team computes a response to it under a time budget, and the response oracle is the team plus its tools.

_**A cheap best response is the deliverable in one project and the disaster in the other.**_ AcceleratePSRO exists to cheapen the oracle, and its deliverable is the crossover point below which imagination stops paying. On the vista page the oracle got cheap without anyone asking, and every downstream quantity that depended on its cost lost meaning. The two pages assign opposite value to the same event, and they are both right, because in the first case the cost of the oracle was overhead and in the second case it was the signal. The general form, worth stating once because it recurs: whenever a quantity is used both as a cost to be reduced and as a measurement to be read, some improvement will destroy the measurement. Effort was the measurement here. Nobody wrote that down before it was gone.

_**A terminated empirical game.**_ The state PSRO stops in: no new strategy the oracle returns improves on the current mixture, so the restricted game has stopped growing and regret against it is zero. Not convergence to the full game, which is what termination is hoped to indicate but does not guarantee; a run also terminates when the oracle is too weak to find an improvement, and the two are indistinguishable from inside the loop. Every team in the competitive tier solving every hard challenge is a terminated game with zero regret. In PSRO that would be reported as a solved restricted game. Read as an instrument, it is the opposite: the analysis has nothing left to say because every entry in the payoff matrix is the same number. The transfer is exact on the arithmetic and inverted on the interpretation, and the reason is that PSRO owns its strategy set while a competition does not.

_**Strategy exploration, read as challenge design.**_ The problem of choosing which strategies enter the restricted game, given that each admission costs a call to the oracle and that the analysis is only as good as the set. Learn Structure supplies the sharp version of the worry: restricting the strategy space is standard rather than cheating, and the restriction error against the full policy space is not bounded. Turned around, this is the design brief for a competition that survives cheap oracles. The challenge set is the strategy set, admission is expensive because authoring is expensive, and the instrument fails exactly when the set stops covering what the entrants can do.

_**Exploitability.**_ How much a player can lose to an opponent who best-responds to them. Not performance against the strategies already present, which is what a scoreboard reports; a profile can top the scoreboard and be highly exploitable. The word transfers to the instrument rather than to the competitors. A test is exploitable when a strategy exists that scores well on it without possessing the ability it was built to detect, and a field that best responds to the test rather than to the domain will find that strategy, without anyone deciding to cheat. Delegation is that best response. So the vista's claim in one line: the instrument was always exploitable and the exploit was expensive until it was not.

The loan stops at three places, and they are worth stating because the analogy is attractive enough to be over-driven. PSRO controls admission to its own population; a competition does not control who enters or what tools they bring. PSRO's payoffs are defined by the game and are stable; a competition's payoffs are authored by people, under time pressure, and the authoring is itself subject to the same assistance the entrants have. And regret in PSRO is computed against a full game that exists in principle, while a competition has no full game to compute against, which is why validity has to be established against an external criterion such as later job performance rather than internally.

## Where these sit on the five axes

Using the axes from the [dictionary overview](../../blogs/cyber-human-ai-dictionary/), for competitive learning in its two senses. **Object**: the behavior of people, and through it their ability, against the strategies of a player. **Epistemic operation**: describe and infer, since the competition is run to find out something already true about the entrants, against enact, since the loop is run to bring the population into existence. **Specificity**: a particular cohort in a particular year on a particular platform, and the whole argument of the vista is that the year matters, against one game indefinitely. **Purpose**: defensive evaluation of a workforce pipeline against strategic response. **Fidelity**: executable, in the sense that the behavior being read is real behavior in a real event, against abstract representation.

The pair sits at opposite corners on four of the five axes and shares only the word. That is a stronger separation than any other collision in this lexicon set, and it is why the phrase gets qualified every time it appears in writing that touches both projects.

## What the vocabulary is for

The measurement problem comes before the pedagogy problem. Until there is an instrument with discriminative power under assisted conditions, no claim about whether assistance helps or harms a cohort can be settled, including the claims on the vista page. Process measurement is the requirement, and it means scoring the solve rather than the flag.

The window for building it is closing on its own. Control-arm attrition is already biasing the studies that would answer the question, and the population of people who can be asked to work unassisted shrinks every year. That is a schedule constraint on the research, not only on the workforce.

And the challenge set has to grow faster than the oracle gets cheap. That is the strategy exploration problem stated for people, with the same unbounded restriction error attached, and it says that authoring is where the effort has to go. A competition that only asks what the field can already do reports nothing about the field, and it will keep reporting it in full confidence.
