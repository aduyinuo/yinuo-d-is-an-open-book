# Research

The craft parts of the job, which are taught by osmosis if at all.

## Reading

**[How to Read a Paper](http://svr-sk818-web.cl.cam.ac.uk/keshav/papers/07/paper-reading.pdf) — S. Keshav.** The three-pass method: five minutes for the shape of it, an hour for the content, and a full pass only for the papers that earn it. Also the best short description I know of how to do a literature survey without drowning.

**Strategies for reading academic articles.** The complement to Keshav: read for the argument's structure rather than front to back, and decide what you want from the paper before opening it.

## Thinking

**[How to Solve It](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691164076/how-to-solve-it) — George Pólya.** Understand the problem, devise a plan, carry it out, look back. The looking-back step is the one everybody skips and the one that compounds.

**[There's more to mathematics than rigour and proofs](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/career-advice/theres-more-to-mathematics-than-rigour-and-proofs/) — Terence Tao.** The three stages: pre-rigorous, rigorous, post-rigorous. Useful well outside mathematics as a description of what expertise actually changes.

**Intuition and logic in mathematics — Henri Poincaré.** A century old, and still the clearest statement that the two are not in competition.

**Finding researchable problems.** The gap between an interesting topic and a question you can actually answer this year is where most projects die.

## Writing

**[The Craft of Research](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo23576265.html) — Booth, Colomb, Williams et al.** Not a style guide. It's about what a claim is, what counts as evidence for one, and what you owe a reader who disagrees with you.

**[Crafting Papers on Machine Learning](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Crafting-Papers-on-Machine-Learning-Langley/3efcb97c1de1c87832a7a1d99e91801992a938ec) — Pat Langley.** What belongs in a well-balanced ML paper, and the different shapes an evaluation section can take depending on what you're claiming.

**[Ten simple rules for structuring papers](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619) — Mensh & Kording.** Structure at three scales — across sections, across paragraphs, within paragraphs. The context–content–conclusion pattern is worth internalizing.

**Headings.** They're the table of contents a reader actually uses. Write them so someone can reconstruct the argument from the headings alone.

**[Garner's Modern English Usage](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/garners-modern-english-usage-9780190491482).** For settling the arguments, and for knowing which arguments aren't worth having.

### Mathematical writing and notation hygiene

**[How to Write Mathematics](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~oxley/halmos.pdf) — Paul Halmos.** Say something, say it in order, say it plainly. Still the best forty pages on the subject.

**[Mathematical Writing](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/klr.html) — Knuth, Larrabee & Roberts.** Notes from Knuth's 1987 Stanford class, with guest lectures from Lamport, Halmos, Ullman and others.

**[On Proof and Progress in Mathematics](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9404236) — William Thurston.** On what mathematicians actually do — advance human understanding — and why the written proof is the residue rather than the substance.

## Presenting

**Presenting in a reading group.** A talk to ten people who will interrupt you is a different genre from a conference talk, and preparing for it as if it were the same is why so many reading-group presentations stall on slide three.

## Running the project

**[NASA Systems Engineering Handbook](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/nasa_systems_engineering_handbook_0.pdf).** Overkill for a research project, which is the point: it names every stage a complex build passes through, so you can decide deliberately which ones you're skipping.

## Venues

**Track relevance.** Before writing for a venue, read its track descriptions and last year's accepted list together. Fit is a property of the pair, and the abstract you write for one track is not the abstract for another.
