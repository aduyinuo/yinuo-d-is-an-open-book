# Present

## The reading group is its own genre

A talk to ten people who will interrupt you is not a short conference talk. The conference talk is a broadcast with questions bolted on at the end; the reading-group presentation is a conversation you are responsible for steering. Preparing the first when you are giving the second is why so many reading-group sessions stall three slides in.

What the genre actually asks for:

**Know why this paper.** Not why it was published — why the group is spending an hour on it. Say that in the first minute.

**Present the argument, not the paper.** You are not obliged to walk the sections in order. Claim, evidence, the one weakness worth arguing about.

**Budget for interruption.** Half your slides is the right amount of material. The questions are the point; if nobody interrupts, the session failed and it was probably your fault for presenting too smoothly.

**Bring the objection yourself.** If you can't name what's wrong with the paper, you haven't finished reading it. Stating the weakness before someone else does changes the room from adversarial to collaborative.

**Have the figure open.** Most productive arguments in a reading group are about one figure or one equation. Know which one it will be.

The MSAIL guide on presenting in a reading group is the short written version of all of this, and it's what I hand to students hosting for the first time.
