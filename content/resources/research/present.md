# Presentation

_**Reading Group**_

[Lead a discussion in a reading group _(by Cathy Wu)_](https://www.wucathy.com/assets/pdfs/wu2012how.pdf)

The single technique that makes the format work, in her words: at the start of the meeting, make everyone ask two questions and write them on a board, and stop people answering them right away, because those questions are the outline for the discussion. "This approach communicates to participants that they are allowed to not understand parts of the paper, but that they must read carefully enough to have questions. This is the secret to making reading groups work."

The baseline to judge a room against: expect at most 50% to participate actively, 20% to talk sometimes, and 30% never. A quiet room is the default, not a failure of the material. Awkward silence is the signal to move to the next question on the board. Keep lexical order through the reading. Watch for the person dominating, yourself included. Close by asking what the group thought of the paper, which doubles as feedback on your own selection.

Choose readings of eight to forty pages. Shorter will not sustain ninety minutes; longer will not be read.

_**Conference Presentations**_

_5-min_

Not the twenty minute talk delivered faster. Only two of the four questions survive at full length: why the problem matters, and the one idea to take home. The method compresses to a single sentence naming the idea, and the evidence to one number under one condition. Roughly five slides. The main message lands by the second slide, because there is no time for a listener to wait for it.

_30-min_

The rules for this are in `internal/slides/TALK-RULES.md`, distilled from Shewchuk and Peyton Jones. A talk is an advertisement for the paper, not a replacement. Four questions every deck answers, and most decks answer only the second. Onion, not clew. Titles carry the message.

_**Poster**_

A poster session is a filtering problem before it is a communication problem. The Better Poster structure inverts the usual layout: a single plain-language statement of the main finding at the center in large type, the traditional sections reduced to a narrow column, and a code linking to the full work. The mechanism is self-selection, so a reader decides in seconds whether to stop.
