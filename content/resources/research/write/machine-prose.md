# Machine Prose

A checklist of habits that mark prose as machine-written, with quantitative thresholds where they exist. Ten are detectable by counting; four need reading.

## Thresholds

* **Em dashes**: 0–2 per 1000 words in human prose; 6–12 or more in machine prose.
* **Negative parallelism** is the single most-cited tell.
* **Sentence length**: machine prose clusters at a 14–22 word median with low variance; human prose bursts. A standard deviation under about 10 is a warning.

## The four that need reading

11. **Defensive disclaimer.** A sentence denying an accusation nobody made. Comes from over-correcting a previous overclaim.
12. **Headline paragraph labels.** An assertion dressed as a heading. A paragraph label names what the paragraph is — Problem, Prior work, Ongoing work — rather than asserting its conclusion.
13. **Register drift in aspiration.** Plans written as accomplishments read as bragging; plans written as wants read as casual. State the commitment or the design.
14. **Integral citation.** "A group at Virginia, led by X, holds an award to…" reads as reporting on someone else's work. The claim is the sentence; the source is a bracket.

## The floor

Not every count should go to zero. A contrast that carries meaning is doing work, and a colon introducing a genuine list is not a tell. A counter finds candidates; a person decides. The number to watch is the trend across passes, not the absolute.
