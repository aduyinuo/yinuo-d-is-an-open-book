# Think

[**How to Solve It**](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691164076/how-to-solve-it) **— George Pólya.**

[**There's more to mathematics than rigour and proofs**](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/career-advice/theres-more-to-mathematics-than-rigour-and-proofs/) **— Terence Tao.**

[**Intuition and logic in mathematics**](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Poincare_Intuition/) **— Henri Poincaré.**

***

All three are about solving. None of them says which problem to take up. That gap is what the notes below work on.

_**Why a list of heuristics is not the deliverable.**_ General heuristics transfer badly. People who can recite them still fail to deploy them, because knowing an operation and regulating when to use it are different capacities. The evidence is consistent that people fail less for lack of heuristics than for lack of regulation: choosing the next move, noticing a line is not working, deciding when to abandon it. So the notes give a procedure with stopping conditions rather than a list of moves.

_**Poincaré: which faculty is in use.**_ "Logic is the instrument of demonstration; intuition is the instrument of invention." Problem finding is invention, so it runs on the faculty that cannot give certainty. That is a reason to write the candidate down and test it, not a reason to distrust it. His sharpest passage is about verification: having checked each operation of a proof one after another, "Shall we have understood it? Evidently not."

_**Tao: when an intuition has earned the right to direct work.**_ Pre-rigorous, rigorous, post-rigorous. The point of rigour "is not to destroy all intuition; instead, it should be used to destroy bad intuition while clarifying and elevating good intuition." An intuition that has survived formalization can be built on. One that has never met the formalism is a candidate, not a direction.

_**Headspace as an instrument.**_ [That page](../../home/what-is-she-thinking.md) has a fixed shape and the shape is the method. Questions written before the answers exist. Sources attached to questions rather than to topics. Questions stated at a level where they could be wrong. Sub-questions that narrow rather than elaborate.

The notes end with a seven step procedure for locating a topic, whose four tests are the regulation layer: write the question so it could be wrong, check that the answer would change something, check that it is reachable this year, and stop when a round of reading adds no new candidate.
