# Procedure

The order of the work matters more than it looks. The window is a few days, and doing a step out of order costs it twice: once to find the right order, and once to redo whatever was built in the wrong one. What follows is the order, and for each step the failure that appears when it is skipped. A step whose failure cannot be named is a habit rather than a procedure, and it can be dropped.

Only one of these steps writes.

## 1. Settle the venue mechanics first

What does the submission form accept? How are words counted, and how is the limit enforced? May new results appear? What revision machinery exists to receive a promise? These answers decide what the draft may contain, which is why they come before any drafting.

Skipping this produces a draft written for a formatted document that then has to be rewritten for a plain text box, or a local word count that disagrees with the site's count in the last hour before the deadline. Losing a response to a mechanical mistake is the worst available outcome, and this step exists to make that outcome unavailable.

## 2. Extract before you argue

Reviewer sentences go into a working file verbatim. Nothing is paraphrased and nothing is summarized. Beside each one goes the manuscript passage it is about, with its line numbers, because that is what decides whether a comment rests on a misreading or names a real defect, and that decision cannot be made from memory of your own paper. Where a reviewer compares the work to a specific prior system, quote that system's own sentences.

Without extraction the draft argues against a remembered version of the review, and it characterizes prior work instead of quoting it. Both failures are invisible to whoever wrote the draft and obvious to the reviewer who wrote the comment.

## 3. Read each reviewer once

Record five things per reviewer. The **score**, which sets how much of the budget that review is worth. The **declared expertise**, which sets the register, since an expert responds to precision and a reader further from the work responds to framing. **What they credited**, which is an asset no concession may spend. **What vocabulary they use**, which is a candidate for borrowing, since their own phrase is the locator that lets them find the answer to their point. **What stake they may hold**, for instance closeness to a system the paper compares against.

Without this the response is three single-reviewer responses stapled together, and it gives up ground that no comment asked for.

## 4. Cluster by root cause, not by topic

The root of a comment is the thing that would have to change for the comment to go away. Comments sharing a root are answered once, as a group, with the group named. Then split any cluster holding two topics a reader would not ask about together, and keep splitting until none does.

A topic label collects everything on its topic and commits the section to none of it, so the section answers three questions at a third of the depth each. That is the failure the ICLR analysis calls inadequate explanation, which carries the largest negative odds ratio in the validated set at 0.31. Grouping is forced by the word limit and by nothing else, so the limit forces grouping and does not license shallowness.

## 5. Classify every comment

Four kinds. A **misreading** is a comment whose premise the manuscript contradicts. A **defect** is one whose premise the manuscript confirms. A **scope question** asks where the boundary of the claim sits. A **request** asks for work.

The kind decides the posture, and deciding it here rather than during drafting is what stops a politely worded comment naming a real defect from receiving a defense. Sorting by which element of the argument the comment attacks is the finer-grained version of the same operation, and it is set out in [Argument](../write/argument.md).

## 6. Gate the commitments

Three questions gate every piece of requested work, in order, and no commitment enters a draft without the author's sign-off.

1. **Was the original choice justified, and is the justification written where the reviewer looked?** If so, the answer is that justification, and no work is promised.
2. **Is the work feasible inside the response window or the shepherded revision?** If not, decline with an honest reason.
3. **Does it require advancement at the scale of a follow-up project?** If so, it is not promised. A response that promises work at that scale concedes that the paper is a major revision at best.

Two rules constrain what leaves the gate.

**A promise names a configuration and a table.** A vague promise about future work is a validated negative, and a shepherd can hold nothing. Where the fix is small enough to fit, give it now instead of promising it: the number itself, or the exact sentence the revision will add. The promise then commits only placement, which is the cheapest promise to keep.

**A decline states a reason that is never effort and never the word limit.** The honest reasons are that the challenge is universal to the field, that the question is open, or that the case sits outside the threat model with the boundary named.

Work you are not sure about becomes an offer rather than a promise. Offers are numbered, each with what it would show, and none is framed as required. The paper should not appear to depend on the reviewers accepting one.

## 8. Run two checks, in order

The **wording pass** runs a checklist mechanically over the finished draft. The habits it catches are invisible to whoever wrote the sentence, which is why it is run as a tool rather than as an intention. The checklist is [Machine Prose](../write/machine-prose.md).

The **per-question audit** is one question repeated over every reviewer question: is this answered? Support it with a listing of every reviewer sentence that no section quotes, then decide one by one which of those are summary or praise and which are gaps. Where a minor point gets no section of its own, a single coverage sentence says the concerns were read, are agreed with, and will be addressed in a revised version. Silence on a reviewer's question, however minor, is what the venues and the evidence both read as evasion.

## 9. Export, count, check, and hold

The plain text is generated from the source rather than kept as a second copy by hand, because a second copy drifts from the first and the drift is discovered at the form. Check that no markup, no working apparatus, no link, and no identifying string survived. Count under the venue's own rule, and treat the site's counter as authoritative. Cut restatement first, then the section answering the least movable review.

The last line of the procedure is the hold. The response goes to the author, not to the form. The author submits.
