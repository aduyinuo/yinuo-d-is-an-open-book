# Research

I work on autonomous agents in cybersecurity, and on the people who have to work alongside them.

The programme splits four ways. Model the machine side of the world well enough to act in it. Model the human side well enough to predict it. Find where the two are genuinely complementary rather than merely co-located. Then ask what it takes to move any of it out of the lab.

## Core research themes

**[Cyber World Modeling](../overview/cyber-world-modeling/)** — what an agent needs to represent about a network and an adversary in order to defend it, and where the strategic structure of the interaction actually sits.

**[Mental World Modeling](../overview/mental-world-modeling/)** — how people solve hard problems, how they read each other in repeated interaction, and how their biases shape what they do. Both for its own sake and because agents have to model it.

**[Human-AI Complementarity](../overview/human-ai-complementarity/)** — teams where the authority, the shared resources and the dependencies are written down explicitly, so that collaboration is something you can measure rather than assert.

**[Toward Deployment](../overview/toward-deployment/)** — what "realistic" means when nobody agrees on it, what training in a realistic environment costs, and whether a policy learned in one place survives the move to another.
