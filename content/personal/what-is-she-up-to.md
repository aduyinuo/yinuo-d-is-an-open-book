# What is she up to?

A publication list tells you what finished. It says nothing about what a week actually contains — which is mostly reading, arguing with a simulator, and rewriting the same paragraph.

So: an activity board. Threads down the side, grouped by the question they serve; kinds of activity across the top. A cell is filled where that thread has left something public behind.

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/activity-board.png" alt="An activity board: research threads down the side, grouped by theme, and kinds of activity across the top"><figcaption><p>Ten threads, seven kinds of work.</p></figcaption></figure>

## The activity types

**read** — literature, standards, other people's code. The unglamorous input.

**model** — writing down the formal object: the value function, the latent, the dependency graph. Deciding what to represent and what to throw away.

**build** — infrastructure. Testbeds, annotation tools, instrumentation, the environment plumbing.

**run** — experiments. With agents, with people, or with both.

**write** — papers, but also the internal documents nobody reads that force the argument to hold together.

**review** — refereeing for venues, and being refereed.

**teach** — lectures, reading groups, mentoring conversations that shaped where a thread went next.
